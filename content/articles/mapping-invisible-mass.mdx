Dark matter remains one of the greatest mysteries of astrophysics. Because it does not interact with electromagnetic radiation, we cannot observe it directly. However, we can map its distribution using **gravitational lensing**.

## Spacetime Curvature and Lensing

According to Einstein's General Theory of Relativity, mass curves spacetime. Light traveling from distant galaxies bends around this curvature. The lensing equation describes the angular position of the image $\theta$ relative to the source position $\beta$:

$$
\theta - \beta = \alpha(\theta)
$$

Where $\alpha$ is the deflection angle. By analyzing the distortions in background galaxies, cosmologists can reconstruct the distribution of foreground mass, exposing the invisible webs of dark matter.
