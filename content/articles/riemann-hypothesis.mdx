Proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, the **Riemann Hypothesis** is one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics, carrying a $1 million prize from the Clay Mathematics Institute.

## The Riemann Zeta Function

The hypothesis concerns the zeroes of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$, which is defined for complex numbers $s$ with $\text{Re}(s) > 1$ by the Dirichlet series:

$$
\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s}
$$

Through analytic continuation, the function is defined over the entire complex plane. The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeroes of $\zeta(s)$ lie on the critical line:

$$
\text{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}
$$

If proven, it would reveal deep connections to the distribution of prime numbers.
